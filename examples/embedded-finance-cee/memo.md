# Embedded Finance Startups in Central and Eastern Europe: Market Map and Shortlist

*Theme:* embedded finance startups in Central and Eastern Europe  
*Generated:* 2026-11-23 · shortlist approved by a human reviewer · fact-checked by a critic model

## Executive summary
This memo maps embedded finance startups in CEE across three segments: embedded lending, embedded payments and checkout financing, and card-issuing and banking-as-a-service (BaaS) infrastructure. We validated six candidates and recommend five. They are Wealthon (Poland), Wallester (Estonia), Flowpay (Czech Republic), Montonio (Estonia) and PayPo (Poland). Paynetics (Bulgaria) was validated but held back because its only sourced funding is a €2.5M round from 2020. The wider backdrop is supportive. Startups in CEE raised €1.83 billion from January to September 2026, up 25% from €1.46 billion a year earlier. Czech startups raised the most capital (€513 million), and Polish startups closed the most rounds (67). The evidence base is thin in places. Our search budget limited us to six validated names against a target of about eight. Several funding figures are unsourced. We found no embedded insurance startups and little on Romania, Hungary or the Balkans.

## Key trends
- Capital is flowing to CEE startups overall. Startups in CEE raised €1.83 billion from January to September 2026, up 25% from €1.46 billion a year earlier. Baltic startups raised €326 million of that, against €506 million in the same months of 2025. Czech startups raised the most capital (€513 million), and Polish startups closed the most rounds (67).
- Embedded lending is attracting large financing rounds that include debt. Warsaw-based Wealthon raised €115.5M in February 2025, including debt financing from Fortress Investment Group and equity from 3TS plus angel investors. The debt and equity split was not found. Prague-based Flowpay secured a €30M facility from Fasanara Capital, described as debt financing.
- Banks are using fintechs as distribution and funding partners. UniCredit signed a three-year agreement with Wealthon for up to PLN 0.5bn (EUR 115M) of SME receivables, as part of its BaaS strategy in Europe. Montonio's checkout financing runs through an exclusive partnership with Inbank. Inbank itself is a licensed bank with an embedded finance platform that works with 6,200+ merchant partners and 847,000+ active contracts across 7 markets. It is also setting up an embedded finance joint venture with Eurobank in Greece.
- Licensed infrastructure is a durable position. Wallester is an Estonian FSA-regulated payment institution and a Visa Principal Member offering card issuing across the EEA and UK. It was named the #1 fastest-growing company in the Fintech, Financial Services & Insurance category of the FT1000 2026. Paynetics is also cited among regulated European embedded finance infrastructure providers.
- Platforms are bundling payments, lending and logistics. Montonio started with bank-link payments and has expanded into card payments, BLIK in Poland, shipping logistics and embedded BNPL or financing. Wealthon pairs POS-turnover financing with a business finance app. The category is broader than BaaS and includes card issuing, payments, lending, payroll and compliance.
- SME lending is a large focus. Tracxn tracks 49 embedded lending companies in Eastern Europe, 22 of them funded. A PragmaGO report claims embedded lending boosts SME growth by up to 36% in Poland. This is a vendor claim and should be treated with caution.

## Recommendations
- Prioritise Wealthon and Flowpay for discussions on embedded SME lending. Wealthon has a UniCredit funding partnership and revenue of just under PLN 30M (EUR 6.9M) in 2025. Flowpay launched in the Netherlands in 2025. Before committing, check how each company's loans are funded, because Flowpay's latest funding is debt and Wealthon's round included debt.
- Treat Wallester as the lead infrastructure option for card issuing. It holds a Visa Principal Membership and an Estonian FSA licence. We found no sourced funding data, so ask for financials and ownership details in due diligence.
- Montonio and PayPo are the merchant-side options. Montonio raised an €11M Series A led by Index Ventures and relies on bank-partnered financing through Inbank. PayPo is described as Poland's largest BNPL provider, but its funding figures conflict across databases, so verify them directly.
- Keep Paynetics on a watchlist. It is a recognised regulated BaaS provider, but its only sourced round is €2.5M from 2020, and we have no evidence of recent traction.
- Close the coverage gaps before any investment or partnership decision. We have no embedded insurance names, and little on Romania, Hungary, Croatia and Serbia. Licensing for Flowpay, PayPo and Wealthon's own operations was not verified, so confirm regulatory status and bank-partner arrangements. Inbank, a licensed bank, is better treated as a competitor or partner than as a startup target.
